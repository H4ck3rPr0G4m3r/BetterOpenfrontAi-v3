# OpenFront AI v3: design

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Status:** Draft for approval
**Predecessor:** [BetterOpenfrontAi](https://github.com/H4ck3rPr0G4m3r/BetterOpenfrontAi) (v1 = PRs #1–#8, v2 = PRs #9–#11). This repo starts clean; v2 stays as reference and as a parts bin.

---

## 1. Intent

An AI for [OpenFront](https://openfront.io) that **holds its own against, and beats, skilled people in public openfront.io FFA lobbies**. Along the way it plays against itself and against its owner (Peter) in local games.

"Good" is judged by results against people: placement in real lobbies, not survival against the engine's nations.

If the OpenFront devs don't allow AI players in public lobbies, the same bar applies in **private lobbies against popular, skilled players** who have agreed to play it.

### Why a third version

v1 and v2 built a working stack: a real headless engine, self-play, replays and live play scaffolding. The AI never got near human level. v3 keeps the parts that worked and changes the two things that capped it:

1. **It learns from strong humans first.** Public games are archived with every player's intents and can be replayed exactly. v3 imitates top-rated FFA players, then improves through self-play. v1/v2 learned from scratch.
2. **It runs on any map and on the live game version.** v2 saw only the World map through a fixed 128×64 grid, on an engine frozen in September.

## 2. Where v1/v2 went wrong, and what was good

### 2.1 What went wrong (evidence from the v2 repo and its run logs)

| Problem | Evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Learning from scratch is too slow for this game.** | MainRunV3_5: survival vs Medium nations went 0% → 17% over ~900k steps at ~160 env steps/s. Win rate around 4% the whole run. | Human level is out of reach on an 8 h/day budget. |
| **Reward whack-a-mole.** | The reward went through v3 to v10 (one revert) and grew to about 20 hand-tuned terms (`crowdedPost`, `boatLaunch` slot rules, `nukeSelf`, `advantage`, …). | Every change made earlier runs incomparable. Behaviour was legislated, not learned. |
| **Late feedback.** | v1 trained 100k steps with actions disconnected (missing `clientID`) before a replay showed it. | Days lost. Bugs were found by reading logs, not by watching. |
| **Action space fought the agent.** | One intent per 1 s step. At 1.2M steps: 41% noop, 9% alliance requests, 3% cancel-attack. Build locations limited to 32 precomputed candidate sites. Hard action rules (troop reserve, no donations or betrayal). | Couldn't express what humans do, and so couldn't learn from them. |
| **No memory.** | Feedforward policy. | It can't remember sent requests, recent attacks or who betrayed whom. |
| **World map only, frozen engine.** | 128×64 raster fitted to World. Engine vendored at `f51f165b` (2026-09-04). Upstream ships several releases a week (v0.35.7 → v0.36.2 in two weeks). | Wouldn't transfer to the public map rotation (106 FFA maps) or to the live version. |
| **Tooling ahead of the agent, and clunky.** | Dashboard, presets, start.bat menu, Playwright lobby driver. Peter: dashboard clunky, presets never used, play-in-lobby clunky locally and blocked by the captcha online. | Effort went into surfaces that weren't used. |
| **Difficulty curriculum muddled the metrics.** | The headline survival dropped at every stage promotion while per-difficulty survival rose. | Curves were easy to misread (a reviewer misread them as collapse). |

### 2.2 What was good (keep the ideas, port code where it fits)

- **Real engine, headless and deterministic.** Seeded PRNG, verified identical hashes. This is the foundation of everything below.
- **Multi-seat games.** Several AI seats in one engine game, so one simulation serves many learners.
- **Node sim workers plus binary bridge to Python** (`bridge/Protocol.ts`, `WorkerServer.ts`, `vec.py`). Proven stable: zero worker deaths over long runs.
- **Intent-first autoregressive heads with pointer networks over entities** (`model.py`). Only the heads an intent uses enter its log-prob.
- **Engine-derived legality** (affordability, buildable types, silo readiness) instead of hand rules.
- **PPO hardening:** Gumbel-max masked sampling (fixes the CUDA multinomial NaN), fp32 heads and value, per-row GAE for multi-seat, aborting games with no learner left, STOP-file and resume.
- **Replays in the real game client.** Peter's favourite part, now a main feature.
- **Death reports** (`DeathReport.ts`): who took the land, when, and why.
- **Metrics** were fine. They move into the app.
- **Honest specs**: measured numbers, corrections recorded rather than rewritten.

## 3. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
1. Imitate top FFA players from public game records, then surpass them through self-play.
2. Play every map in the public FFA rotation, on the current live engine version.
3. One desktop app for everything: training sessions, data, replays with an "AI mind" overlay, local play, online play.
4. Play against Peter locally without a browser hack, and in openfront.io lobbies through Peter's own browser.
5. Frozen benchmarks, so every model ever trained is comparable.

**Non-goals (for now)**
- Team modes (planned after FFA works; the design must not block them).
- Special lobby modifiers (random spawn, compact, starting gold, doomsday clock, …). They are skipped in data at first.
- A small distilled or browser-sized model. Not considered in this design, and it places no constraints on it (no export format, no op restrictions).
- Matching the engine's nations.

**Anti-goals: failure even if it "works"**
- A reward that grows a term every time a behaviour looks wrong.
- Anything that solves, bypasses or hides from the captcha or other human checks. A human always passes them.
- An AI in public lobbies that pretends to be a person. It always plays under a visible tag (e.g. `[AI] name`).
- Crawling the API aggressively. One request per second at most, back off on errors, stop if asked.
- Long training runs nobody can watch. Every milestone produces something watchable.

## 4. Constraints and facts

- **Hardware:** 16-core CPU, RTX 3060 Ti 8 GB, Windows 11. Training happens in **~8 h sessions** (PC left on during school). Everything trains in resumable sessions.
- **Cloud (optional, later):** if results are good, rented GPUs train bigger models. Whatever is trained must still **run** on the 3060 Ti. Training code must run headless on Linux with CUDA (no app needed there). Recommendation: **Vast.ai** (cheapest marketplace) or **RunPod** (simpler). Imitation is GPU-bound, so rent a single 4090-class card for hours. Self-play is CPU-bound (simulation), so rent many vCPUs, not a bigger GPU. Check prices when renting.
- **Engine:** [openfrontio/OpenFrontIO](https://github.com/openfrontio/OpenFrontIO), AGPL-3.0. Our code links it, so this repo is AGPL-3.0.
- **Upstream release cadence:** several releases a week, 100+ commits a month.
- **Online:** openfront.io requires a Turnstile captcha on join. The local dev server skips it (`Worker.ts`: `ServerEnv.env() !== GameEnv.Dev`). Whether OpenFront allows AI players in public lobbies is **pending Peter's conversation with the devs**. Online play is built behind that answer. If the answer is no, online play is used only in private lobbies with players who agreed.
- **Online code stays private** (§17), so the public repo can't be used to flood openfront.io with bots.

### 4.1 Measured facts about game records (2026-11-14)

- Public endpoints used by the game client: `GET api.openfront.io/leaderboard/ranked?page=N` (lists `1v1`, `2v2`, `ffa`, `ffaSeason` with elo and `public_id`); `GET /public/player/{publicId}/games` (cursor-paginated history: gameId, type, mode, map, duration, players, result, `ffaRating`); `GET /game/{gameId}` (the full `GameRecord`).
- One 34-minute, 58-player FFA record: **160 KB on the wire** (gzip), 1.05 MB JSON, fetched in 0.6 s.
- Record contents: `gitCommit` (exact engine commit), `info.config` (map, bots, nations, modifiers), `info.players` (clientID, username, **publicID**), `info.winner`, `turns` (empty turns omitted). 20,332 turns in 2,040 s = **10 turns/s**. A **state hash every 10 turns**, so replay correctness can be checked.
- That game had 8,873 intents: attack 3,967, build_unit 2,059, upgrade 947, boat 892, allianceRequest 318, allianceExtension 148, move_warship 36, cancel_attack 16. The most active player made 919 intents (~0.45/s average, in bursts). Turns with 2+ intents are common (1,600 of 7,419 recorded).
- Maps: 118 in total, **106 in FFA rotation** (World weight 20, the rest 1–10). Sizes from 512×512 to 4432×2528, with portrait maps (2036×4428).
- Note: the record above was a **private** lobby from a top player's history. Top players' histories mix team, private and public games, so the crawler filters.

## 5. Approaches considered

**A. Copy first, then improve (chosen).** Behaviour cloning on strong players' records, then self-play RL with a pull toward the cloned policy. Simple, mostly terminal reward.
- Pro: starts at human-like play. The reward stays simple. It uses the one resource v1/v2 lacked.
- Con: weeks of infrastructure (crawler, multi-version replay, intent translation) before there is an AI to watch.

**B. v2 path, fixed.** Pure self-play with a better action space, memory and reward.
- Pro: most code reuse, fastest first result.
- Con: v2's own data says it is too slow to reach human level on this budget.

**C. Scripted skills plus a learned commander.**
- Pro: decent fast, interpretable.
- Con: capped by the scripts, which skilled humans already exploit in nations.

Peter chose **A**.

## 7. Engine and versions

This is the hardest infrastructure piece and it comes first.

- `engine/` is a **git submodule** pinned to the release live on openfront.io. Updating is an app action: "Update engine", which bumps the submodule, builds, runs `sim/` tests and replays a few fresh records with hash checks.
- **Version cache.** Every record names its `gitCommit`. `engines/<commit>/` holds a built bundle of `sim/` compiled against that commit (a `git worktree` of the submodule plus install plus an esbuild bundle). Records are replayed by the bundle of their own commit.
- **Version window.** Only commits inside a window (default: the latest ~10 live commits, or the last ~3 weeks) are built and crawled. Older records are skipped rather than supported. This keeps the number of builds small and the data close to the current game.
- **Narrow engine adapter.** `sim/` reaches the engine only through one adapter module, so upstream API drift is fixed in one place. A commit whose adapter build or hash check fails is dropped from the window and logged, not patched around.
- **Determinism check.** Replaying a record compares our state hash with the record's hash every 10 turns. Any mismatch rejects the whole game from the dataset.
- **Records are complete for replay.** The engine seeds its PRNG from the game ID (`GameRunner.ts`: `new PseudoRandom(simpleHash(gameStart.gameID))`). Map, bots, nations and modifiers are in `info.config`, and spawns are ordinary `spawn` intents in `turns`. The game client's own replay viewer works from exactly this record, so nothing else is needed.
- Open risk: deployment commits may not equal release tags. M0 checks first that record commits resolve upstream. Games whose commit doesn't resolve, or doesn't build, are dropped (counted per commit). If most recent games fall into that bucket, M0 stops and the plan is revisited.

## 8. Human data pipeline

### 8.1 Crawl (`learn/data/crawl.py`)

1. **Teachers.** Pull the `ffa` and `ffaSeason` leaderboards (all pages), plus players seen with high `ffaRating` in crawled games. A teacher is a `publicID` with FFA rating above a threshold (default: top ~200 FFA players; tunable).
2. **Games.** Walk each teacher's history (cursor pagination). Keep: `type == Public`, `mode == Free For All`, duration ≥ 5 min, no special modifiers (checked on the record's `info.config`), `gitCommit` in the version window.
3. **Records.** Fetch `/game/{id}` at **≤ 1 request/s** for the whole crawler, with exponential backoff on 429/5xx and a generic User-Agent (no personal data). Store as `data/records/<commit>/<gameId>.json.gz`.
4. **Index.** SQLite `data/index.sqlite`: games (id, commit, map, players, duration, winner, config flags), players (publicID, best known rating), seats (game, clientID, publicID, placement, teacher flag). The crawl is resumable and incremental.

Targets: **2,000 games** for the first imitation milestone (~1 h of crawling), **20,000** for scale (~6–8 h, ~3 GB). The crawl can run during training sessions, since it is network-bound.

**Volume check (sampled 2026-11-14):** four players from the FFA top 50 had 3, 19, 32 and 37+ public FFA games of 5 min or more in the 3 weeks before. Two of them hit the sampling page cap, so they had more. Top players often share lobbies, so unique games are fewer than the sum. Expectation: a 3-week window over ~200 teachers gives roughly 2–4k unique games, which is enough for M3. **20k needs more:** either a wider version window (more commits to build; decided in M8 from M0's measured adapter churn) or a lower teacher threshold (any seat above a rating cut-off counts as a teacher, not only leaderboard players). The crawler reports unique games per window and threshold so the choice is made on numbers.

### 8.2 Replay to samples (`sim/replay/`)

A Node worker loads a record into its commit's bundle and steps the real engine turn by turn, checking hashes. At each **decision step** (default every 5 turns = 0.5 s) it encodes the observation for each **teacher seat** and attaches that seat's intents from those 5 turns, translated into action labels (§10.4).

**Default: stream, don't store.** Observations are large, and storing millions of them would take hundreds of GB. Instead, the replay workers feed the trainer live, like rollout workers with no policy. Estimate: World simulates at ~1,000 ticks/s and one sim serves several teacher seats, so 14 workers should keep up with a 3060 Ti. The builder measures this and adds an on-disk cache for a hot subset only if the GPU starves.

Non-teacher seats can also be encoded for value pretraining (every seat's final placement is known). That's the builder's call, weighed against throughput.

### 8.3 Sample weighting

- By teacher rating (higher-rated players count more).
- By outcome (the seat's final placement in that game), so a top player's bad game counts less.
- STOP/no-action steps are down-weighted so the policy isn't dominated by "wait".

## 9. Observation (any map)

The same encoder runs in training, in record replay and in the headless client. It is written in TypeScript against the engine adapter.

The AI sees the game the way a strong player reads it: **who** matters first (players and their borders), then **where**, in sharp detail only where something is happening. v2 squeezed World's 2000×1000 tiles into one fixed 128×64 grid (~16×16 tiles blurred per cell, World-shaped only). Nothing in v3 depends on map size or shape.

```
 minimap (whole map)            zoom windows (sharp)
 ┌───────────────────┐          ┌──────┐ ┌──────┐ ┌──────┐
 │      ░░▓▓         │          │front │ │front │ │coast │ ...
 │   ▓▓▓▓▓▓██  ▒▒    │          │ vs A │ │ vs B │ │      │
 │  ▓▓[me]▓▓██▒▒▒    │          └──┬───┘ └──┬───┘ └──┬───┘
 └─────────┬─────────┘             └───────┬┴────────┘
           │        players + border links │   units / attacks
           ▼                ▼              ▼          ▼
         ┌──────────── combine (attention) ───────────┐
         └──────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                     LSTM memory
                            │
   action type → target player / window+cell / minimap cell / unit
```

### 9.1 Players and border links (the main view)

- **Player tokens** (the ~32 most relevant players: neighbours, the biggest, anyone attacking us; plus one pooled "rest" token): tiles, land share, troops and max troops, gold (where visible to players), relative strength vs us, kind (human/nation/bot), relation flags (ally, pending request either way, traitor, embargo, alliance expiry), whether alive.
- **Border links:** for each pair of players that share a border (us ↔ neighbour, *and* neighbour ↔ neighbour), the border length, troop density on each side near the border, terrain along it (plains/highland/mountain mix, as it affects combat), whether they are attacking each other and with how many troops, plus the distance by sea when there is no land border.
- The links enter the attention as edge features, so the AI can see that "A and B are busy fighting each other, so B's other border is open". v2's flat player table had no relationships, so it couldn't.
- Most decisions happen here: an attack targets a **player**, not a tile.

### 9.2 Minimap (the whole map, always)

- The whole map resampled to a fixed grid (default 128×128), keeping its shape (portrait maps stay portrait), letterboxed, with a validity plane.
- **Planes:** land/water/coast; own, allied, hostile and unowned land; troop density own/hostile; structures by type (own/hostile); boats, warships, nukes in flight (own/hostile); hostile SAM cover; land of players attacking us, and of players we attack.
- Used for the big picture: where to spawn, where to boat across the sea, where to nuke.

### 9.3 Zoom windows (sharp where things happen)

- Up to **8 small windows**, default 32×32 cells at a fixed **2–4 tiles per cell** (the scale is given to the model), with the same planes as the minimap.
- **Placed automatically and deterministically** from the game state, so replay and live play place them identically:
  1. our core territory;
  2. each active front (borders where we, or they, are attacking), largest first;
  3. the best coastal launch areas;
  4. incoming nukes.
  Unused windows are masked out.
- Every window goes through the **same** small CNN and becomes a token (with its position and scale). The model learns through attention which window matters now.
- Cost doesn't depend on map size: a 4432×2528 map costs the same as a 512×512 one.
- Window size, count, scale and placement priorities are tunables (§20). The translator report (§10.4) shows how often human actions fall inside a window, which is the test of the placement rule.

### 9.4 Units and actions in progress (lists)

- Own structures (type, level, position, which window it's in) for Upgrade.
- Own warships and boats at sea.
- Outgoing and incoming attacks (for CancelAttack), with troops and target.
- Incoming alliance requests and alliances close to expiring.

### 9.5 Globals and memory

- **Globals:** time, spawn phase, gold, troops and max troops, growth rate, counts, affordability per buildable, game config flags (nukes, SAMs, alliances), map size and minimap scale.
- **Memory:** an LSTM core (§11), so it remembers what it just did, recent attacks, and who betrayed whom.

### 9.6 General rules
- **No map identity** is fed in, so the policy generalizes across the rotation.
- **No fog of war.** OpenFront shows every player the whole map, so a full observation is what humans see too. UI state (camera, attack slider) is not modelled: the slider shows up in the troops of each intent, which the labels capture.
- **Shared per-game work:** in multi-seat games, everything seat-independent (terrain, ownership raster, unit positions) is computed once per step and only the perspective is applied per seat. v2 found encoding, not simulation, was the main cost.

## 10. Action space (mirrors real intents)

### 10.1 Timing

One decision step every 5 turns (0.5 s, configurable). In each step the policy emits **0 to K actions** (default K = 4) autoregressively, ending with STOP. This matches human bursts (several intents in one turn) without wasting a step on each.

**Latency.** Records already carry human latency: intents sit on the turn the server received them. Live, the AI decides on the latest turn it has and its intents land a turn or more later. To match, self-play applies each step's intents after a random delay of 1–3 turns (configurable). Live clients act on turn boundaries; if a decision is still running when the next step is due, that step is skipped, not queued.

### 10.2 Action types

| Type | Arguments |
|---|---|
| STOP | – |
| Spawn | location |
| Attack | target player entity or "unowned land"; troop fraction |
| Boat | location; troop fraction |
| Build | unit type (city, port, factory, defense post, SAM, silo, warship, atom bomb, hydrogen bomb, MIRV); location |
| Upgrade | own structure entity |
| MoveWarship | warship entity; location |
| CancelAttack | outgoing attack entity |
| CancelBoat | own boat entity |
| AllianceRequest / AllianceReject / AllianceExtension / BreakAlliance | player entity |
| TargetPlayer | player entity |
| Embargo start/stop | player entity |
| DeleteUnit | own unit entity |

Excluded for FFA: emoji, quick chat, donations (teams later), lobby/admin intents.

**Nothing is banned by hand rules.** Legality comes from the engine. Habits like keeping a troop reserve, or not breaking alliances, are learned from humans and then from outcomes.

### 10.3 Arguments

- **Troop fraction:** 20 buckets of 5% of current troops. Humans use a percentage slider, so the label is `troops / current troops` at that moment, bucketed. `troops: null` maps to the client's default ratio.
- **Location: two ways to point.**
  - **Near (inside a zoom window):** pick a window, then a cell in it. At 2–4 tiles per cell this is near tile-accurate. Used for builds, defence posts, nearby boat landings and warship moves.
  - **Far (on the minimap):** pick a minimap cell, then a fine spot inside it from a zoomed crop around that cell computed on demand. Used for boats across the sea, nukes, spawn, and anything outside every window.
  - Either way, the decoder snaps to the nearest legal tile (engine checks: buildable, valid boat destination, …). A location that can't be snapped is dropped and counted, never silently retargeted (v1's retarget bug).
  - Human labels use the near path when the tile falls inside a window, and the far path otherwise.
- **Entity pointers:** attention over the relevant entity set (as in v2): players, own structures, warships, boats, attacks.
- **Masks:** type-level legality from the engine every step (can afford, has ready silo, has a warship, …). Window and minimap cell masks are cheap (land, own territory, coast, hostile land for nukes). Exact placement relies on snapping, not full per-tile masks.

### 10.4 Human-intent translator

`sim/translate/` maps every teacher intent to a v3 action (`attack` → Attack with target and bucketed fraction; `build_unit` → Build with type and tile → window + cell, or minimap cell + fine spot; `upgrade_structure` → Upgrade with the entity index; …). There is a round-trip test: translate, decode back to an intent, and compare with the original. Exit gate: **≥ 98% of teacher intents representable**, with location error after snapping under a small tile threshold. Unrepresentable intents are counted by type and reported.

## 11. Model

- **Encoders:**
  - minimap: a residual CNN to a feature map (kept for the far pointer) plus a summary token;
  - zoom windows: one shared residual CNN, each window becoming a token (plus its feature map, kept for the near pointer), with position and scale embeddings;
  - players: tokens through a transformer whose attention uses the border links as edge features;
  - units and attacks: small MLPs into tokens;
  - globals: an MLP.
- **Fusion:** a few transformer layers over all tokens (minimap summary, windows, players, units, globals), pooled into the core input.
- **Core:** an LSTM (default 512). This is the memory v2 lacked. Imitation trains on sequences (truncated BPTT, sequence chunks of e.g. 64 steps).
- **Heads:** action type → arguments, autoregressive within a step and across the K actions of a step.
  - Entity pointers (players, structures, units, attacks, windows) by attention from the core.
  - Cell inside a window from that window's feature map, conditioned on the core (FiLM).
  - Far location from the minimap feature map, then the fine spot from the on-demand crop.
- **Value:** predicts the final placement/win probability. It is pretrained during imitation on known human outcomes, so RL starts with a sensible critic.
- **Size:** default **~10–15M parameters** on the 3060 Ti (v2: 0.76M).
  - Rough split: image CNNs ~3M, player/border attention and fusion ~2M, LSTM ~3M, heads ~2M.
  - Why this size: an 8 h session should push ~30–40M samples through it, about one full pass over the first 2–4k games, which fits that much data without memorizing it. Bigger would mean fewer passes per session.
  - Cloud models (~30–60M) only once the 20k-game dataset exists. Any model must run live on the 3060 Ti at 2 decisions/s per seat, which is trivial at these sizes.
  - Measure samples/s at M3 and adjust.
- **Format and inference:** plain PyTorch checkpoints. No export format is chosen now. Live play (the headless client) asks the app's Python service for actions over a local socket, one batched call per decision step for all AI seats it runs. No op restrictions.

## 12. Training phase 1: imitation (behaviour cloning)

- **Loss:** cross-entropy per head on teacher actions (§8.3 weights), plus value regression on final placement.
- **Split:** by game (never by step). A fixed held-out set of games is frozen at milestone start.
- **Offline metrics:** per-head top-1/top-5 accuracy, location error in tiles, STOP precision/recall, value calibration.
- **Behavioural metrics (what matters):** the frozen benchmarks (§14), plus "land curve vs teachers": the BC agent's land share over time compared with teachers' curves on the same maps.
- **First-milestone target (absolute, since v2 can't run on v3's maps and observation):** survive 20 min vs Medium nations in at least 70% of benchmark games (v2 on World: ~17%), place top 3 in at least 50% of the Hard-nations benchmark, and look human-like in replays. The numbers are starting targets, adjustable once real data exists.

## 13. Training phase 2: self-play RL

- **Algorithm:** PPO with recurrent state, ported from v2's core (Gumbel-max sampling, fp32 heads, NaN guards, per-row GAE, abort learner-less games), plus a **KL penalty toward the frozen BC policy**, annealed down over training. Small entropy bonus.
- **Reward:** terminal placement (final rank among all players) plus a win bonus, and **at most one** shaping term: potential-based change in land share. **Rule:** a new reward term is only added if a benchmark A/B shows it helps. Benchmarks don't depend on reward, so this can be checked.
- **Games look like public FFA lobbies:** rotation maps, public-lobby bot and nation counts (from crawled configs), and many policy-controlled seats:
  - the learner (several seats);
  - **league opponents:** frozen past snapshots, chosen to keep head-to-head near 50%;
  - **human stand-ins:** frozen BC policies sampled at varied temperatures, so the learner keeps facing human-like play and doesn't overfit to itself.
- **Throughput:** many AI seats per sim amortize the simulation. Shared per-game encoding (§9) amortizes the encoder. Measure and record steps/s per seat and per game at milestone start (v2 baseline: ~160 env steps/s, ~240 rows/s).
- **Sharing one PC:** a session reserves CPU cores (default: all but 2) and the GPU. The app stays responsive during a session: replays and metrics are viewed after the fact, and starting a local game pauses the session (or shrinks it to fewer workers) until the game ends. Sessions mostly run while Peter is away, so contention is rare.
- **Sessions:** a training session has a start, a planned end ("until 15:30") and clean resume. On return, the app shows a session summary: benchmark deltas, rating change, a few auto-picked replays (best win, worst loss).

## 14. Evaluation (frozen benchmarks)

Frozen at the start of milestone 3 and versioned (`bench-v1`). Benchmarks never change meaning. A new benchmark gets a new version.

1. **Solo vs Hard nations:** 1 AI seat with public-lobby bots and Hard nations, 12 fixed maps × fixed seeds. Metrics: placement, survival at 20/30 min, land share curve.
2. **Human-proxy lobby:** 1 AI seat vs ~20 frozen BC copies (the BC model frozen when `bench-v1` is created) plus nations and bots, on the same maps and seeds. Placement.
3. **Head-to-head ladder:** every saved checkpoint gets a rating (TrueSkill or Elo) from multi-seat FFA games against other checkpoints.
4. **Imitation set:** held-out human games, offline metrics (§12).
5. **The real metric:** every local and online game against people (public lobbies, or private lobbies with skilled players who agreed) is logged with the AI's placement and the players' ratings where known. The app shows this as its own chart.

Death reports (ported from v2) are attached to every benchmark game where the AI dies.

## 15. Playing

### 15.1 Shared core: headless client (`sim/client/`)

A Node program that is a real game client without a window:
- connects over the game's own WebSocket protocol;
- receives turns and runs the matching engine bundle locally, the same way the browser client keeps state;
- encodes, gets actions from the Python inference service (§11) and sends intents back through the protocol.

The local and online paths differ only in transport. The AI's name always carries the `[AI]` tag.

### 15.2 Local games

The app starts the engine's dev server in the background (no captcha in Dev). In **Play → Local**:
- host a lobby (map, nations, bots);
- add N AI seats (choose a checkpoint per seat);
- open the real game client inside the app (or in the browser) to play yourself;
- or spectate an all-AI game live.

No Playwright, no browser automation.

### 15.3 Online games (milestone 7, gated on the devs' answer, private repo)

A browser extension (Chrome MV3) for Peter's own browser. It lives in the private repo (§17). If the devs say no to public lobbies, it is used only in private lobbies with players who agreed.
1. Peter opens openfront.io, joins a lobby and passes the captcha himself.
2. In the extension's panel, he clicks **"AI takes over"**. The content script forwards the page's turn stream to the app over a localhost WebSocket. The app runs the same headless-client core (engine bundle for the site's current `gitCommit`, built on demand). Intents go back through the page's own transport.
3. **"Hand back"** returns control at any moment. A visible in-page badge shows when the AI is playing.

The extension never touches the captcha or any human check.

## 16. The app (`app/`)

An Electron app. Node is already required by the engine, it can embed the real game client for replays and play, and it spawns the Python service. One double-click opens it (a desktop shortcut or portable build), and background processes (Python service, game dev server) start and stop with it. No .bat menus.

Built with the `frontend` skill and real design effort. Not a form page.

| Screen | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Home** | current best model and its rating; the running or last training session (time left, summary); data status; quick actions (Start session, Play, Watch latest) |
| **Train** | start or stop a session with a planned end time; phase (imitation/RL); live metrics; benchmark ladder; session history |
| **Data** | crawl progress, games per map and version, teachers, version window, "Update engine" |
| **Replays** | library of AI games (training samples, benchmarks, local/online) and human games. Opens in the embedded real client with the **AI mind overlay**: value/win estimate over time, top-k candidate actions with probabilities, the chosen action drawn on the map. Compare an AI game with a teacher's on the same map |
| **Play** | Local (host, add AIs, play or spectate) and Online (extension status, take over / hand back, game log) |
| **Models** | checkpoints, ratings, benchmark results |

States that must exist: empty (no data or no model yet), loading/progress, error with a fix hint (e.g. "dev server failed on port 9000"), and running jobs that survive a window close.

**Replay sidecar:** AI games record the engine's `GameRecord` (as in v2) plus a sidecar with per-step policy outputs (value, top-k actions and probabilities). The overlay needs this.

## 17. Public and private code

The goal is that nobody can take this repo and flood openfront.io with bots.

- **Public repo (this one):** everything else, including the headless client with its **local** transport only (it joins dev-mode servers, which have no captcha).
- **Private repo** (`BetterOpenfrontAi-v3-online`, created at M7): the browser extension and the online transport that connects it to the headless-client core. It depends on the public repo's `sim/client` as a library, so there's no duplicated logic.
- **Trained checkpoints are not published.** They are gitignored here. Sharing a model is a deliberate, separate decision.
- The app's Online tab only appears when the private package is installed next to it. The public app works fully without it.
- AGPL note: AGPL obligations trigger on distribution or offering a network service. Keeping the private repo for personal use is fine. If it is ever given to others, its source goes with it.

## 19. Decisions made in this design (Peter can override)

- New repo; v2 untouched.
- Engine as a submodule plus a per-commit version cache, with a version window.
- Python for learning, TypeScript for anything touching the engine; Electron for the app.
- Streaming replay into training instead of a stored dataset.
- 0.5 s decision step, up to 4 actions per step.
- Location by window+cell (near) or minimap+fine spot (far), with engine snapping.
- LSTM core; plain PyTorch checkpoints, with live inference through the Python service (no export format yet).
- Online play code in a private repo; checkpoints not published.
- KL-to-BC regularized PPO with placement reward and at most one shaping term.
- Frozen benchmark suite as the only yardstick across versions.
- The online AI always tagged `[AI]`; a human always passes captchas.

## 21. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Engine API drift across versions breaks the adapter | narrow adapter; version window; drop failing commits |
| Record commits don't resolve upstream | check in M0 before anything else depends on it |
| API rate limits, or OpenFront objects to crawling | ≤ 1 rps, backoff, resumable; stop and ask the devs if told to |
| Bots not allowed in public lobbies | online play is gated behind the devs' answer; local play and private lobbies with friends who've agreed don't depend on it |
| BC learns "average" behaviour | teacher filter plus rating/outcome weighting; RL phase |
| Streaming replay too slow for the GPU | measure in M3; cache a hot subset |
| RL drifts away from human-like play | KL to BC; human-stand-in opponents; imitation metrics tracked during RL |
| Large maps slow simulation | measure per-map tick rates in M2; weight map sampling by both rotation weight and cost |
